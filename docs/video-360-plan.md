# 360 video — plan

**Phase 1 built; phases 2–5 open.** Written 2026-08-28, then largely *answered* the
same day by reading `F:\workspace\rust\HMDViewer`, which already ships 360 video
over passthrough on a Quest 3. Mono first; **stereo is the destination**,
because this gets tested on a headset.

## What it is

Equirectangular video projected onto the inside of a sphere with the viewer at
the centre — the Las Vegas Sphere arrangement. The video *is* the world around
you, not a screen in it.

## A payload, not a preset recipe

`XrdsSceneNodePayload::Video360`, spawning its own sphere, rather than a palette
preset that drops a Sphere node with the right boxes ticked.

Two reasons, and the second is the one that settles it:

- **"An inverted unlit sphere with flipped U and a radius of 100" is not an XRDS
  concept.** The project's own test is whether a non-expert can use a feature
  through XRDS concepts alone. A `360 Video` node passes; a recipe does not, and a
  recipe is also four things an author can silently un-tick.
- **Stereo layout has nowhere to live on a generic sphere.** Over-under packing is
  a property of the *clip*, not of the mesh, and there is no field on a Sphere
  payload to put it. A preset would have to grow into a payload the moment stereo
  landed.

## Mono, and no stereo field yet

**Reversed 2026-08-28.** This section originally said to ship `stereo_layout`
immediately with `Mono` as the only honoured variant, so no migration would be
needed later. That reasoning was wrong: `#[serde(default)]` already makes adding a
field free, which is the whole point of it — so pre-placing one buys nothing and
costs something real, namely a control an author can set that does nothing. This
project has spent enough on authorable-but-inert fields to not add one on purpose.

So there is no stereo field. It goes in when stereo does.

Stereo is a per-eye UV transform — each eye samples a different half of the frame.
That reaches into the XR render path, which cannot be prototyped on a desktop, so
it is deliberately last. The reference implementation is mono, so it is also the
one phase with nothing to copy.

## What phase 2 built

`XrdsSceneNodePayload::Video360` → `XrdsVideo360` → `spawn_video_360_descriptor`,
with the sphere generated by `video_sphere::inside_facing_equirect_sphere`.

The runtime sets unlit, `AlphaMode::Blend` and the video's slot itself rather than
leaving them to the author, because each is invisible when wrong *in the same way*
— a black view — and none is distinguishable from the others in a headset.

`Blend` is set even at opacity 1.0. Resolving to `Opaque` at spawn would mean an
author lowering opacity afterwards saw nothing change, which is the failure this
whole feature is aimed at.

Five tests, and the one that matters is `every_face_points_inward`: it was checked
against a deliberately reversed winding and fails there, because an outward sphere
is not subtly wrong from inside — it is *entirely invisible*, and indistinguishable
from a missing texture, an unplayed video or a wrong radius.

## Four things the sphere needs that a normal mesh does not

**1. Interior rendering, which is half-built.** `XrdsMaterialPbrParams.double_sided`
exists and reaches Bevy at `material.rs:136`, but `cull_mode` is left at its
default `Some(Face::Back)` by the `..default()` on the next line. In Bevy those are
independent: `double_sided` flips normals so back faces *light* correctly;
`cull_mode` decides whether they are *drawn*. So ticking double-sided today fixes
the lighting of a face you still cannot see.

**Use `cull_mode: Some(Face::Front)`, not negative scale.** Flipping the sphere
with a `-1` X scale is the classic Unity trick and it fixes the mirror for free,
but negative scale inverts winding globally and this project has already been
bitten once by an entity whose `Aabb` did not match what was drawn
(`NoFrustumCulling` blocking `Aabb`, which silently broke grab and raycast). A
render-state flag stays in the renderer where it belongs.

**2. The horizontal mirror.** A texture seen from behind reads backwards. Fixed by
flipping U in the mesh's UVs — engine-side, never authorable, because there is no
case where an author wants their 360 video mirrored.

**3. Unlit.** Already in the schema. A 360 backdrop lit as a diffuse surface would
be dark and would catch scene lights, which is not what a video is. Note that unlit
and *opaque* are separate: over passthrough this stays unlit and stops being
opaque — see below.

**4. Out of the fog, and inside the far plane.** A backdrop must not fade with
distance, and a large radius has to stay inside the camera's far plane or it
disappears entirely.

## The viewer does not move — answered 2026-08-28

The sphere is static. A 360 video scenario puts the viewer at the centre and keeps
them there; that is the normal case and it is the intended one here. So no
camera-parenting, no anchor, no position-follow, and the trap that would have come
with it — a sphere that rotated with the head, making it impossible to look
around — never arises.

Radius still has to sit inside the camera's far plane, and large enough that
roomscale drift does not carry a standing viewer near the wall.

## The real target: 360 video **over passthrough**

This is the app. It changes what the sphere has to be, and the change is not small.

**The compositing side is already built and already correct.** Worth stating
plainly, because it is the part that would normally be missing:

- `passthrough.rs` sets the camera's clear colour to `Color::NONE` when passthrough
  is on, so wherever the app draws nothing, alpha is 0.
- `projection.rs` flags the layer `BLEND_TEXTURE_SOURCE_ALPHA |
  UNPREMULTIPLIED_ALPHA` while passthrough is submitting, and its comment records
  why the second flag matters: Bevy renders straight alpha, and without the flag
  the runtime assumes premultiplied and darkens every partially transparent pixel
  toward black instead of revealing the room.

So **the app layer's alpha is what reveals reality**, and that machinery works.

**But the sphere as planned defeats it.** An opaque sphere surrounding the viewer
writes alpha 1 across the entire view. Passthrough is beneath it and never shows.
"Unlit and opaque" was written for the VR case and is wrong for this one.

### Answered by HMDViewer, which already ships this

`F:\workspace\rust\HMDViewer` is the reference implementation the video spike was
built from, and its **phase 4 is done and verified on a Quest 3**: 360 video over
`XR_FB_passthrough`, passthrough submitted beneath the projection layer. It is a
driving/teleoperation app — the operator sees the vehicle interior *through* the
video. So this is not a design question any more, it is a transcription job.

**It uses uniform opacity, and nothing cleverer.** Default **0.8**, made
runtime-configurable per device (`files/video_opacity.txt`) because the right value
is something you tune in the headset, not something you can reason out. The
fragment shader is three lines:

```glsl
o_color = vec4(texture(u_video, v_uv).rgb, OPACITY);
```

**So chroma key and packed alpha are out**, and the packed-alpha-versus-stereo
collision never has to be resolved. Neither is needed for this app, and neither is
worth building on speculation.

The lesson for our side: **opacity must be authorable and easy to change.** A
constant compiled into a shader would have been the obvious first cut and is
exactly what HMDViewer found insufficient.

### The mesh is built inside-facing, not culled inside-facing

HMDViewer's `sphere_mesh()` generates an **inside-facing** sphere directly, with
equirectangular UVs baked into the vertices and no normals at all (interleaved
`[pos, uv]`, five floats — it is unlit, so normals would be dead weight).

That is better than this plan's original route of a normal sphere plus
`cull_mode: Front` plus a U flip, and it makes two of the "four things the sphere
needs" disappear: winding handles the interior, and the UV formula handles the
mirror. Phase 1's `cull` field stays worth having for *authored* meshes — a plane
readable from behind, an interior a designer models — but `Video360` should
generate its own geometry rather than lean on render state.

**Copy the orientation convention exactly**: `u = 0.5` faces **−Z**, `v = 0` at the
top of the video. The seam lands behind the viewer and the video's centre is
straight ahead, which is what an author expects and is not something to
rediscover by eye.

### A punch-through surface — missing from this plan entirely

HMDViewer has a second thing this plan did not account for: a **world-anchored
punch-through window**, an alpha-0 quad that clears a region back to *fully clear*
passthrough. In its case, so the operator can see the physical monitor in front of
them through an otherwise video-filled view.

Its shader writes `vec4(0.0)` — colour zero, alpha zero — and the compositor then
shows passthrough at full clarity there.

This is a separate authorable thing from the sphere, and it is the difference
between "the room is dimly visible everywhere" and "this rectangle is really the
room". Worth a payload of its own; it is a small one, and nothing about it is
video-specific.

## What phase 3 built

Palette entry under Scene, a `Video360` inspector section (clip, radius, opacity,
play-on-load), the `SetVideo360Params` command, and the DTO both ways.

**The part worth recording is the binding rule, which had a hole in it.** A video
is one clip to one surface — a decoder is a thread on a desktop and a hardware
codec session on a headset, so two meshes sharing one would have to play in
lockstep. That rule already existed, and both the document validator *and* the
editor's picker implemented it by walking **material texture slots**. A `Video360`
keeps its clip in the payload instead, so both were blind to it: two spheres could
have shared a clip, and the picker would have offered a bound clip as free.

Fixed in both, and in that order — the document first, because it is the
authoritative gate. That ordering is the lesson from the video texture slot, where
the runtime resolver and the picker were both fixed and the validator was missed.

Two new refusals came with it: a `Video360` naming an asset the catalog does not
have, and one naming an asset that is not a video. The second matters more than it
sounds — a texture on a 360 sphere renders as a still image, which looks exactly
like a video that will not play.

Five tests, including the two that must *not* fire: two spheres with their own
clips is fine, and an empty clip id is fine because placing a node before importing
its video is normal authoring.

## Two bugs first use found, both silent

**A shader error on every draw.** Our prepass shader called
`pbr_input_from_standard_material` unconditionally, and that reads
`in.world_normal` — a field `prepass_io.wgsl` puts behind
`NORMAL_PREPASS_OR_DEFERRED_PREPASS`. In a depth-only prepass it does not exist,
so pipeline specialisation failed with `invalid field accessor world_normal`. The
mesh was checked first and *does* carry normals; it was never the cause. The
depth-only path now builds only what the alpha discard needs, with the bindless
branch copied verbatim from `bevy_pbr::pbr_prepass_functions`.

It surfaced with `Video360` because that material is `AlphaMode::Blend` even at
opacity 1.0 — deliberately, so lowering opacity later works — which is what routes
it through the discarding prepass. The bug was latent for anything that got there.

**Track-preview Stop left the video playing.** The fourth time in this project that
code walked material texture slots and missed a video kept somewhere else. `Play`
worked because it reads the *runtime entity*, which does get a material at spawn;
`Stop` reads the *document*, where a `Video360` has no material at all.

Fixed with one accessor rather than a fourth patch:
`XrdsSceneDocument::video_asset_ids_on_node` knows both places a video can live,
and its doc comment names all four failures so the fifth person finds it.

## Phases

Reordered 2026-08-28 once HMDViewer answered the open questions. Passthrough
blending moves up — it is a per-node opacity value, not the research project it
looked like — and stereo goes last because it is the only part with no reference.

| Phase | Scope | State |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | `cull_mode` plumbed from the material | ✅ built — for authored meshes; `Video360` does not depend on it |
| 2 | `Video360` payload + runtime spawn: inside-facing sphere generated directly, equirect UVs with `u=0.5` at −Z, unlit, mono, **authorable opacity** | ✅ built |

## What stays out

- **Cubemap / skybox routing.** Bevy's skybox needs a cubemap, so this would mean
  converting video to a cubemap every frame. The equirect→cubemap machinery exists
  for IBL, but that is a one-time bake at import; per-frame it is the expensive
  wrong path.
- **Chroma key and packed alpha.** Ruled out above: the reference app needs
  neither, and packed alpha would collide with stereo's frame packing.
- **180° video and fisheye projections.** Same shape, different mesh and UVs;
  additive when something asks.
- **Ambisonic audio.** 360 video often ships with head-locked spatial audio. That
  is a separate subsystem and not a rendering question.
